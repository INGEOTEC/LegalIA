The Instruments of the Atlas, as md2akn Reads Them

315 laws, 864 regulations, 125 guidelines and 1,373 treaties, article by article

This page opens every instrument of the Atlas at the single snapshot its vectors were built from and shows it the way the project reads it, where the consolidated text is cut into a hierarchy of books, titles, chapters and articles, and each article into its paragraphs, points and subpoints, and every node carries a small badge with its type, its identifier and its number. The aim is to let anyone check that reading by eye, to find the places where it goes wrong, and to report them from the page itself.

A badge names a node in the vocabulary of Akoma Ntoso, a standard for legal documents, and hovering over it gives the Mexican term for it. What the vocabulary calls a paragraph is a fracción in a Mexican law, a point is an inciso, and a level is an apartado. The Raw button of any node shows the exact Markdown that node was read from, and the Raw mode of the whole page shows the document once, with every excerpt underlined and any character that belongs to no excerpt marked in red.

The outline on the left follows the same hierarchy. An annex that the Court’s text numbers after a chapter, such as Annex 2-A of chapter 2, is shown under that chapter, and an appendix under the annex or the instrument that it closes, each one named by its own kind, so that Annex 2-A and Appendix 1 are never mistaken for each other.

An excerpt is the piece of text that the Atlas compares, which is one article, one heading, one introduction, one table, one stretch of loose text or the preamble, each with its own vector. An introduction is the text that a title, chapter or section opens with before its first article or its first subdivision. A table is a run of rows that the consolidated text carries outside any article, and the page draws it as a table; a long one is cut at a row boundary into several excerpts, each of which has its own address made of the table’s identifier and the number of the part. Not every excerpt is compared with the rest of the corpus, because a heading and a transitory article say little about how two instruments relate, so only the remaining ones, called compared excerpts, carry nearest texts. Opening the Nearest texts of one lists the five closest texts that live in other instruments, each with its similarity, and a text that several instruments share appears once with the others named under it. The similarity is the one given by the Qwen3 Embedding 4B model, the 4B option of the Atlas, and following a link opens that other instrument at the excerpt in question.

Seven flags describe what the reading found. A flag is a count and never a judgement, since many of them are legitimate in a given law, and the table at the top ranks the instruments by their sum so that the likeliest problems come first.

  • Loose is text that no rule placed inside an article.
  • Repeated number marks two articles that ended up with the same identifier outside the transitory section, that is the same number in the same place of the outline, so that the second could only be kept apart with a suffix. The same number in two different containers, article 1 of Part 1 and of Part 2, is not a repeat.
  • Number gap is an article whose number does not follow the previous one in its own container.
  • Over window is an excerpt longer than the embedding model's window, whose vector was computed on a truncated text.
  • No articles means the instrument has no article at all.
  • Uncovered counts the characters of the document that fall in no excerpt, no annotation and no frontmatter.
  • No transitory section says only that the instrument has none.

The documents themselves are not part of the site’s source. They are the atlas-instruments release of the repository, which the publishing workflow downloads and unpacks next to this page, and the page fetches one file only when an instrument is opened. An address such as instruments.html?c=leyes&k=ccf#art_1 opens an instrument and an excerpt directly, and the browser’s back button returns to the previous one.
