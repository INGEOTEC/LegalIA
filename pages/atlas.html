An Atlas of Mexican Federal Law: Laws, Regulations and Guidelines

315 laws, 1,082 regulations and 126 guidelines, placed by where their provisions’ closest texts live

Every federal law, regulation and guideline the Supreme Court publishes in consolidated form is one point on this map. Two instruments sit close together when the texts nearest to their provisions live in the same places — the organic laws of the public universities, for instance, land beside one another, whatever words they happen to use.

How to read the map

A point

One law, regulation or guideline. Its area is proportional to its provisions (articles, transitory articles and the other pieces it is divided into), and its colour is its collection: blue for laws, green for regulations, magenta for guidelines.

Distance

Two instruments sit together when their provisions’ closest texts live in the same places, not when they use the same words. The map is a picture of where federal law borrows from and repeats itself.

The numbers

Each provision was matched to the single closest text outside its own instrument, over 381,349 distinct texts. A match shared by m instruments gives 1/m to each, so an instrument’s matches add up to its provisions.

Search for an instrument or click a point: the panel lists the five instruments its provisions are closest to, with how many of its provisions went to each, and the five instruments whose provisions point at it hardest. Lines on the map join it to its five closest instruments, numbered as in the panel; a name in the panel selects that instrument, so the relations can be followed from one instrument to the next. Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan, and press Escape or click an empty spot to clear the selection. The collection buttons under the map show one collection at a time.

An example

The LEY que crea la Universidad Autónoma Chapingo has 35 provisions. For 13 of them, the closest text outside the law belongs to the LEY Orgánica de la Universidad Autónoma Agraria Antonio Narro; for 11.3, to the LEY Orgánica de la Universidad Autónoma Metropolitana (a decimal is a match shared between several instruments); 2 go to the REGLAMENTO INTERIOR DEL COLEGIO DE POSTGRADUADOS, and one each to the LEY Agraria and to the LEY Federal de Producción, Certificación y Comercio de Semillas. The remaining 6.7 are spread over other instruments, and on the map the law sits among the organic laws of the UNAM, Antonio Narro and the UAM. The relation is directed: 28 provisions of other instruments have their closest text in Chapingo’s law, 9 of them in Antonio Narro’s and 7 in the UAM’s.

The neighbourhood size

The map is drawn four times from the same data. The neighbourhood size is how many neighbours each instrument is placed against when the map is drawn: small values keep tight local groups, such as the universities above, and large values keep the overall shape, such as how the laws, the regulations and the guidelines sit relative to one another. Switching it changes the layout, never the relations the panel lists.

Data. The texts are the three consolidated corpora this project publishes from the Supreme Court (SCJN) — laws (scjn-leyes), regulations (scjn-reglamentos) and guidelines (scjn-lineamientos) — divided into provisions and embedded with Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B. The map is a UMAP projection, cosine metric, of each instrument’s matches. The Diario Oficial de la Federación remains the official source of legal text. Map data generated on 22 September 2026.
